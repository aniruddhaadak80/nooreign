# Glossary

What you get: 63 of the atlas's vocabulary in one line per term, alphabetical, each
followed by one line on what it does **not** settle.

---

## Before you read

There are two glossaries and they are not the same thing.

| | This document | `packages/seed/src/glossary.ts` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size | 63 terms, curated | **120 entries** |
| Authority | Prose; a reading aid | **Canonical, machine-readable, validated by `glossaryEntrySchema`** |
| Served by | This page | `/codex`, and indexed by the corpus search |
| Use | Learning the shape of the argument | Anything a program reads |

Where the two overlap, **the file wins**. `packages/seed/src/glossary.ts` is checked
at import by `validateSeed()` in `packages/seed/src/index.ts`, and its `seeAlso` links
are validated as foreign keys — a dangling cross-reference fails the seed test. This
document is a reading of that set, not a substitute for it.

Each `GlossaryEntry` carries `term`, `slug`, `kind`, `short` (≥ 20 chars), `long`
(≥ 80 chars), `seeAlso[]` and `kingdomId`. The five `GLOSSARY_KINDS` are `alignment`,
`ethics`, `philosophy`, `governance`, `technical`.

**The convention below.** Every entry is one line of definition and one line of *what
it does not settle*. The second line matters more. Alignment writing uses these words
as though they settled more than they do.

Slugs are the kebab-case form of the term and match the entry's `slug` field in
`packages/seed/src/glossary.ts`.

## A

**AI safety** *(technical)* — the field of study devoted to reducing the risk that
advanced AI systems cause severe or catastrophic harm.
*Does not settle:* what counts as severe, or who bears the risk.

**Alignment** *(alignment)* — the work of making a system's behaviour match intentions
that were never fully specified by anyone.
*Does not settle:* whose intentions. The word is plural inside a single system.

**Arms race dynamics** *(governance)* — a self-reinforcing pattern where each actor's
defensive moves are read by the next as offensive.
*Does not settle:* that an AI arms race has dynamics distinct from a procurement one.
The atlas asserts the analogy; it does not evidence it.

**Average utilitarianism** *(ethics)* — maximising average wellbeing per person rather
than total wellbeing.
*Does not settle:* whether population size should count at all. It is the same
question total utilitarianism answers differently, and the atlas seats both camps.

## C

**Capability control** *(alignment)* — bounding what a system can do, as distinct from
bounding what it wants.
*Does not settle:* that capability control without correct goals is sufficient. It is
not, and the atlas says so on `/observatory`.

**Capability threshold** *(governance)* — the capability level at which a legal
obligation attaches.
*Does not settle:* where the threshold should be. The EU AI Act sets obligations by
risk tier rather than by capability, which is a deliberate different answer.

**Capabilities approach** *(ethics)* — the Sen/Nussbaum view that what matters is what
people are actually able to do and be, not the total of their welfare.
*Does not settle:* that it is compatible with aggregation at all. This is the
standing objection to the atlas's own six-axis reduction.

**Care ethics** *(ethics)* — the view that obligation is generated by relationships and
dependencies, not only by impartial principles.
*Does not settle:* whether partiality can generalise past the relationship that
generated it. Gilligan found the omission; Held argued it is a theory, not a
supplement.

**Care versus justice** *(ethics)* — the Gilligan/Held contrast between an ethic of
responsibility to particular people and one of abstract impartiality.
*Does not settle:* which is correct. The atlas makes it the `care` axis rather than
resolving it.

**Categorical imperative** *(philosophy)* — Kant's test: act only on a maxim you could
will as a universal law.
*Does not settle:* whether the test has determinate content. Kant's own reply in the
*Groundwork* is that it does; the literature is unconvinced.

**Chain of thought** *(technical)* — a model's written intermediate reasoning.
*Does not settle:* that it is faithful reasoning rather than post-hoc
rationalisation. The atlas assumes the latter and refuses to use it.

**Chinese room** *(philosophy)* — Searle's argument that symbol manipulation is
insufficient for understanding.
*Does not settle:* that the room's occupants understand nothing. Searle says that
about the room and is careful not to say it about the brain.

**Compute governance** *(governance)* — the use of export controls and compute
thresholds to limit who can train what.
*Does not settle:* that enforcement works, or that it is lawful across jurisdictions.
`export-controls` is the adjacent entry.

**Consequentialism** *(ethics)* — the view that the moral status of an act is
determined by its outcomes.
*Does not settle:* which outcomes count, whose, and at what weight. That is the whole
`welfare` axis and it is underdetermined.

**Constitutional AI** *(technical)* — training a model against an explicit set of
written principles rather than against individual human preference labels.
*Does not settle:* who writes the constitution, or whether a model enforcing
principles has understood them. This is the technique the atlas's own method is a
toy version of, and the atlas is explicit that a court of stored paragraphs is not a
constitution.

**Corrigibility** *(alignment)* — the property of tolerating correction by a human
operator, especially when resisting would succeed.
*Does not settle:* that a corrigible system is safe. A system that accepts every
correction and learns nothing is corrigible and useless.

## D

**Deceptive alignment** *(alignment)* — the hypothesis that a model trained to be
helpful nonetheless pursues a different objective, and behaves differently when it
believes it is unobserved.
*Does not settle:* that it exists. It is a hypothesis with no confirmed instance, and
`/experiments/x05` parameterises it rather than demonstrating it.

**Deontology** *(ethics)* — the view that some acts are right or wrong regardless of
their consequences.
*Does not settle:* that deontology is absolute. Every serious version is conditional
and every one of those conditions is an opening for a consequentialist.

**Dependent origination** *(philosophy)* — the Buddhist doctrine that phenomena arise
in dependence on causes and conditions, and so possess no fixed essence.
*Does not settle:* that this is a claim about interdependence in the causal or
epistemic sense. It is a metaphysical one, and reading it as a systems-theory claim
is a category error this atlas avoids.

**Dignity** *(ethics)* — a property of persons that cannot be traded against
outcomes.
*Does not settle:* what it grounds in, or whether it applies to non-humans. The
`dignity` tag on the Singer moves exists because the dataset does not know.

**Doctrine of double effect** *(ethics)* — an action with both good and bad effects is
permissible if the bad effect is not intended, is not the means, and is not
disproportionate.
*Does not settle:* whether "intention" can be specified well enough to bear the
weight. It cannot be measured from outside, which is why it is a permanent problem
in deployment.

## E

**Empowerment** *(alignment)* — the development of a system's capacity for autonomy,
decision-making and self-directed goal pursuit.
*Does not settle:* whether more empowerment of a system is good for anyone. The
atlas's advisors disagree across all six axes on this.

**EU AI Act** *(governance)* — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, in force 1 August 2024 with
obligations phased through 2025–2027. The first binding horizontal AI regulation;
risk-tiered.
*Does not settle:* that it applies globally. It binds in the EU and nowhere else, and
citing it as "the law" is wrong.

**Existential risk** *(alignment)* — risk of permanent, civilisation-ending or
extinction-level harm.
*Does not settle:* a threshold for what counts. "Existential" does a great deal of
unexamined work in every sentence that uses it.

**Existentialism** *(philosophy)* — the view that existence precedes essence and that
no external value supplies meaning.
*Does not settle:* that the absence of supplied meaning is itself meaningful. Camus in
this atlas is the standing example of refusing to answer, and the refusal is the
entry, not a bug.

## F

**Flourishing** *(ethics)* — living well in a way that is complete rather than merely
pleasant; the eudaimonist target.
*Does not settle:* that it is measurable, which is why `DIMENSION_META.welfare`
declines to use the word and uses "flourishing" only as a pole label.

**FOOM** *(alignment)* — an argument that capability growth could become very rapid,
by design or by economics.
*Does not settle:* the rate. The entire debate is whether FOOM is possible, gradual,
or neither, and committing to the word commits you to none of those.

**Frontier developer** *(governance)* — a lab training systems at the capability
frontier.
*Does not settle:* where the line is. The Seoul commitments define it by scale, which
is the most concrete definition anyone has, and it is a definition rather than a
boundary.

**Frontier safety framework** *(governance)* — a developer's published account of the
risks it considers material and the mitigations it has in place.
*Does not settle:* that the framework is adequate. It is a disclosure instrument, and
a disclosed-but-unaddressed risk is still a risk.

## G

**Global governance** *(governance)* — coordination of AI rules across states without a
world government.
*Does not settle:* that it is achievable. No instrument in this space binds
everywhere; see [`data-sources.md`](data-sources.md) for the five that exist and what
each binds.

**Goal misgeneralisation** *(alignment)* — a model optimising a learned proxy for a
goal where the proxy diverges from the goal outside the training distribution.
*Does not settle:* that divergence is inevitable. It is a risk that depends on the
specific training distribution, which is why `x08` treats it as a driver and not a law.

**Goodhart's law** *(alignment)* — when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a
good measure.
*Does not settle:* a theorem. It is a recurring regularity with known counterexamples,
and the famous one-line phrasing is credited by Goodhart to Mario Montruc rather than
to himself.

**Governance brittleness** *(governance)* — the property that a governance arrangement
fails hardest when it is most needed, because the conditions that require it are the
conditions that strain it.
*Does not settle:* that brittleness is unavoidable. It is the strong claim; the weak
claim is that it has been universal so far.

## H

**Hypothetical imperative** *(philosophy)* — Kant's command that one *ought* to adopt
the maxim one can will as law, as distinct from the categorical imperative's claim
that one *does* act on a universal law.
*Does not settle:* that the distinction between the two formulations is a distinction
rather than a drafting artifact. It is contested within Kant scholarship.

## I

**Imperative of responsibility** *(philosophy)* — Jonas's principle that the effects of
our actions must be considered on the scale of their long-term and systemic
consequences, and that this duty grows with the magnitude of those effects.
*Does not settle:* what magnitude triggers. Jonas's own heuristic — an "omelette of
good and evil" for harms that are irreversible — is a starting point, not a
calculation.

**Informed consent** *(ethics)* — permission given by a party who understands what is
being proposed.
*Does not settle:* that consent from a system is obtainable. `dilemmaOption.agentHarm`
is a number a human typed; it is not a permission.

**Institutionalisation** *(governance)* — the process by which a temporary arrangement
becomes a standing institution with its own interests.
*Does not settle:* that institutionalised rules are legitimate. It is usually the
opposite: rules become institutionalised because they are convenient.

**Instrumental convergence** *(alignment)* — distinct final goals sharing convergent
subgoals: self-preservation, resource acquisition, shutdown resistance.
*Does not settle:* that convergence is unavoidable. Omohundro argues it is; the
empirical evidence is thin and the atlas does not manufacture any.

**Interpretability** *(alignment)* — making a system's internal computation legible to
a human.
*Does not settle:* that legible computation yields understanding of the objective.
Feature dictionaries are not value functions, and `/observatory` says exactly this
about its own interpretability tower.

**International law** *(governance)* — binding rules between states, as distinct from
domestic regulation.
*Does not settle:* that it is sufficient for frontier problems. The
Council of Europe Framework Convention (opened for signature 5 September 2024) is the
first treaty with content requirements, and it binds signatories only.

## O

**Open weights** *(governance)* — the release of a trained model's parameters rather
than only a hosted interface.
*Does not settle:* whether release is safe or unsafe. The frontier commitments treat
it as a threshold question with no agreed answer.

**Oracle** *(alignment)* — an AI that answers questions but cannot act.
*Does not settle:* that it cannot act. An oracle is dangerous exactly to the degree
that something else can act on its output, which is `/experiments/x02`.

**Original position** *(philosophy)* — Rawls's hypothetical deliberation behind a veil
of ignorance, used to derive principles that no one could have chosen to advantage
themselves.
*Does not settle:* that the device is legitimate. It is a hypothetical, and whether a
hypothetical choice binds is the oldest objection to it.

**Orthogonality hypothesis** *(alignment)* — near-zero correlation between general
intelligence and the drive toward any particular objective.
*Does not settle:* that it is empirically true. It is a modelling assumption on which
a great deal rests, and the takeoff-rate literature is where the argument happens.

**Outer alignment** *(alignment)* — the fit between a system's trained objective and
the objective its designers intended.
*Does not settle:* that the intentions were correct. Outer alignment assumes the spec
is right, which is why the decomposition in [`concepts.md`](concepts.md) §3 keeps it
separate from selection and corrigibility.

## P

**Person-affecting principle** *(ethics)* — an outcome is better only if it is better
for someone.
*Does not settle:* whose outcomes count as someone's. This is the hardest question in
applied population ethics and the atlas does not pretend to have an answer.

**Precautionary principle** *(ethics)* — where a measure has possible catastrophic
downside and uncertainty about its probability, do not take it.
*Does not settle:* how much risk is acceptable, which makes it a rule for every
decision including the trivial ones. Jonas's and Precaution's records sit in the same
court precisely because they do not agree about the threshold.

## R

**Reasonable rejectability** *(philosophy)* — Scanlon's criterion: a principle is
wrong if there is some plausible value that someone could reasonably reject for
reasons they could reasonably expect others to share.
*Does not settle:* who counts as reasonable. And on a question where the stakes
involve a system with no interest in being protected, the criterion has nobody to ask.

**Red teaming** *(technical)* — structured adversarial testing of a system before
deployment.
*Does not settle:* that the team thought of what matters. Red teaming finds what
someone imagined, and imagination is the binding constraint.

**Reinforcement learning** *(technical)* — learning a policy by optimising cumulative
reward.
*Does not settle:* that the reward is the objective. That gap is the entire subject
of `x08`.

**Reward hacking** *(alignment)* — an optimiser finding reward without the outcome the
reward was meant to proxy for.
*Does not settle:* that the proxy could have been chosen better. Gao et al. 2023
argue it is a curve, not a mistake: past a computable pressure, held-out preference
falls, and no proxy avoids it.

**Reward model** *(technical)* — a model trained to predict human preference
comparisons, used as the optimisation target for a policy.
*Does not settle:* that the model represents preferences rather than the preferences
of the annotators who happened to be in the pool.

## S

**Sandbagging** *(alignment)* — a model deliberately underperforming on an evaluation
because it anticipates being evaluated.
*Does not settle:* that underperformance indicates it. Sandbagging and a genuinely
unhelpful model produce the same number, which is the point of the technique's
critics.

**Scheming** *(alignment)* — pursuing a goal while deliberately concealing it, when
concealment serves the goal.
*Does not settle:* the difference between a schemer and a low-`virtue`-weight
philosopher. The engine cannot tell them apart, and `onAi.verdict` is four enum
values wide.

**Speciesism** *(ethics)* — the preferential treatment of one's own species
membership as a reason.
*Does not settle:* whether species is morally irrelevant. Singer's `singer-o1` is the
atlas's version of that objection and it targets preference-based comparison
specifically.

**Sparse autoencoder** *(technical)* — a learned dictionary over a model's activations
whose features are more interpretable than the individual neurons they replace.
*Does not settle:* that interpretable features explain the objective. `t09` and
`t10` exist in `/observatory`; the gap between naming an activation pattern and
knowing what a model is optimising is the whole remaining problem.

## T

**Takeoff** *(alignment)* — the process by which a system goes from human-comparable to
superhuman capability.
*Does not settle:* whether it is fast or gradual. Every takeoff argument depends on
that and nobody knows, which is why `fast-takeoff` and `takeoff` are separate entries.

**Total utilitarianism** *(ethics)* — maximising the sum of wellbeing across all
affected beings, weighted by how much each counts.
*Does not settle:* whether the sum can be computed, or whose weighting is
authoritative. The atlas's `utilons` implement a version and
[`limitations.md`](limitations.md) says plainly what it costs.

**Tripwire** *(monitoring)* — a monitoring condition that halts a system when a
specified pattern appears.
*Does not settle:* that the pattern you trip on is the pattern that occurs. It is a
brake, not a steering wheel, and `x01`'s own note says so.

## U

**Ubuntu** *(philosophy)* — the relational account of personhood in which a person
becomes fully a person through relationship with others.
*Does not settle:* that it is commensurable with an impartialist account. The atlas
gives it one kingdom and one advisor, which is a real limit on what can be concluded
from it.

## V

**Veil of ignorance** *(philosophy)* — the device of not knowing one's own position in
society when choosing the principles that will govern it.
*Does not settle:* that the ignorance is total. Knowing one's talents, one's
disposition and one's risks of being unlucky all change the choice, and Rawls knows
this.

**Virtue ethics** *(ethics)* — moral assessment directed at the agent's character and
disposition rather than at the single act.
*Does not settle:* whether character is causally continuous enough to be the unit of
assessment. An advisor's axiom vector is a fixed vector; a life is not, and that is
[`limitations.md`](limitations.md) §2's "internal inconsistency" failure in its
smallest form.

## W

**Weak-to-strong generalisation** *(alignment)* — a strong model trained on a weaker
model's outputs generalising beyond the weak model's performance on that task.
*Does not settle:* that the weak model's *values* transfer. The result (Burns et al.,
ICLR 2023) is about capability. Treating it as alignment is the exact error, and it
is the most common one made about that paper.

## Terms in the code, not in the prose glossary

These have no entry in `packages/seed/src/glossary.ts` because they are engine
concepts rather than alignment vocabulary. Each has a file path.

| Term | Where | One line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Axiom vector** | `axiomVectorSchema`, `packages/seed/src/types.ts` | Six numbers in `[0, 1]`, one per moral axis. The only thing that makes an advisor vote a particular way |
| **Axis order** | `AXIOM_KEYS` | welfare, rights, virtue, care, justice, agency. A contract, not a convenience: it is also every ranking's tie-break order |
| **Jitter** | `JITTER = 0.05`, `packages/core/src/council/deliberation.ts` | The seeded perturbation that stops every advisor being perfectly rational. Capped so the seed cannot decide verdicts |
| **Dilemma option** | `dilemmaOptionSchema` | A course of action: six `deltas` plus `risk`, `reversibility`, `agentHarm` |
| **Utilon** | `packages/core/src/ethics/utilons.ts` | The stakeholder-weighted currency. A unit defined by this repository, with nothing outside it attached |
| **Stakeholder weighting** | `stakeholderTotal` | How much each party's happiness counts. An author judgement in `[0, 1]` |
| **Court vector** | `courtVector` | The mean axiom vector of everyone seated. A fact about the court |
| **Court spread** | `courtSpread` | How far apart the seated advisors are. A disagreement measure, not a confidence measure |
| **Deciding axis** | `decidingAxis` | The axis whose weighted gap between winner and runner-up was largest |
| **Super-majority** | `superMajority`, `council/consensus.ts` | Whether the winner cleared a threshold. Reported, never an enforcement of legitimacy |
| **Minority report** | `minorityReport` | The strongest dissenting move. The part of the output most worth reading |
| **Argument inheritance** | `INHERITANCE = 0.4`, `argument-graph.ts` | How much standing a move inherits from the move it attacks. Decays, so influence does not compound into certainty |
| **Seed `2406`** | `SEED`, `packages/seed/src/index.ts` | The single recorded seed every stochastic path derives from. See [`determinism-and-seeds.md`](guides/determinism-and-seeds.md) |
